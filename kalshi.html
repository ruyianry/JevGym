JevGym · Kalshi component

Jev vs. the market on Kalshi

A timestamped Kalshi benchmark and trading arena. Every prediction is made with only the information that existed at the time, then scored against what actually happened — calibration and realized profit.

Why Kalshi first? Its markets quote a direct probability for every event — continuously updated, and directly comparable to Jev's probabilistic output (probability in, probability out). JevGym isn't limited to Kalshi; the benchmark will be populated from many sources. Kalshi is simply the cleanest first case study.

Why it matters

Readjusting the market toward the truth

A market price is just the crowd's probability that something happens. When the crowd is off and a model is right, that gap is a mispricing — and a model that sees it can help nudge the price back toward reality.

We don't aim to extract arbitrage — JevGym gives models a chance to readjust the market. The headline metric is still reward: it measures how much a model would move the price toward the truth (its edge vs the crowd, held to resolution). Being accurate is nice; being accurate where the market is wrong is what moves prices.

Built to be forked — in the spirit of nanoGPT and llama.cpp: small, readable, runs offline on your laptop, and adding a model is one small adapter.

How it works

State → bet → reveal

For every resolved market we build snapshots at fixed horizons before it closes and at fractions of its lifetime. Each snapshot separates the public state, the market state, and the outcome — the renderer decides what a model sees.

① State→ ② Bet→ ③ Reveal→ ④ Score

Two tracks — blind (no price) and market-aware (sees the crowd) — plus the arena, where the model trades its edge and is scored on realized P&L. The eventual outcome is the only oracle.

The leakage rule. Every piece of evidence carries a public-availability timestamp, and available_at ≤ snapshot.timestamp is enforced. When an agent searches the web, results are bounded to the same moment via the Internet Archive — it reads the past, not the future.
Case study · Weather preliminary

Jev outperforms the market on Kalshi

Our first case study is weather — Kalshi daily-temperature markets. Jev matches the crowd on the clearer markets and trades them at a profit 🤑, with real room to grow on the hard ones. This is one category — more to come.

First live run: jev-1.13.0 on 72 real, already-resolved markets (720 held-out checkpoints). Every bet uses only pre-resolution state; then the outcome is revealed and scored. Reward (arena P&L, real $$) is the headline; Brier is the calibration check.

Profit by difficulty — easy to hard

"Difficulty" is how uncertain the market itself is: a near-0/near-1 price is easy, a coin-flip price is hard. Arena return is realized P&L per $1 contract.

Before the market has a price+26.9%
  • Profitable across every difficulty band — and it earns most where the market is least sure.
  • Before a contract is even priced, Jev already has signal the crowd doesn't.
  • Arena overall: +11.8% average return per contract (49 wins / 20 losses).
New — a multi-day horizon (preliminary). Beyond the one-shot bet above, the arena now also replays a ~1-week window where Jev re-forecasts and re-bets each day, compounding conviction as the price drifts. Early live runs show it behaves like leverage — it amplifies Jev's edge where it has one (rate-threshold markets lift further with the horizon) and, honestly, compounds losses on the hard markets where it doesn't (storm counts). Full multi-day tables are being finalized preliminary.

No leakage

Keeping it honest

A large model can memorize outcomes that were public before it was trained. So any LLM-backed forecaster is scored only on markets that resolve after its public release date plus a buffer (three months by default), or after one global cutoff for a strict head-to-head. Anything a model could have trained on is dropped before scoring.

The dataset

KalshiJev — the Kalshi subset of the JevGym dataset

KalshiJev is the Kalshi subset of the JevGym dataset (more sources to follow as their own subsets). Markets normalize into one schema with configs for markets, price_history, evidence, and snapshots. Splits are chronological and grouped by event.

Build it yourself. KalshiJev is a spec, not a data drop — the tools reproduce it from data you fetch under each source's Terms of Service. The repo ships code only and republishes no operator data; Kalshi's Data Terms restrict training and redistribution of their data without prior written consent. See the disclaimer.
Run it

Offline in one command block

The whole pipeline runs offline on bundled sample data — no keys, network, or GPU.

pip install -e ".[dev,hf]"

jevgym ingest kalshi --from-fixtures
jevgym parse && jevgym build-snapshots && jevgym validate
jevgym eval --agents kalshi_market,mock --track both

Set a key in .env to benchmark Jev live on real resolved markets:

jevgym eval --agents kalshi_market,jev --track both

Other System-One models plug in the same way — one small adapter each.
